The honest answers

A few things you might be wondering.

When will it launch?

There isn’t a launch date yet. We’re using this waitlist to see if people want the idea before building the full service. Joining means we can send you updates and early access details if we move forward.

Can I upload a bill or donate now?

No. This is an early concept page. Please don’t send bills, account numbers, payment details, or money. We’ll explain the process if bill submissions or payments open in the future.

Can I ask for help before a bill is overdue?

Yes. The concept includes bills that are coming due as well as past-due bills. A request would show the due date so givers can understand the timing, but joining this waitlist does not guarantee that any bill will be funded before its due date.

How would the money reach someone?

After a bill is reviewed and the recipient is verified, the concept is to send the approved funds to that person’s verified personal account. It would not have to go to the utility or other biller. A later update could distinguish money sent from a bill the recipient confirms they paid. No payments are available on this page.

Would a payment be tax deductible?

We haven’t established a final legal or tax structure. Don’t assume payments would be tax deductible. We’ll make the terms clear before accepting any payment.

Can givers get public recognition?

That’s a core part of the idea. Profiles could show funds sent and bills recipients later confirm as paid, with totals tied to verified transactions. Givers could choose a public name or anonymity, share impact summaries, follow one another, and build giving circles.

Can people follow a giver or a bill campaign?

That’s the idea. People could follow a giver’s public profile or a specific campaign and choose whether to receive updates about new verified requests, campaign progress, and confirmed impact. Following would not mean agreeing to donate, and follower growth would never be guaranteed.

Can sharing help more people see my request?

That’s the concept. People could share a verified request link, helping more givers discover it and the recipient’s public profile. Recipients would choose what personal details to show. More sharing may increase potential visibility, but it cannot guarantee views, donations, or funding by a bill’s due date.

Can businesses choose whether to show gratitude posts?

That’s the idea: each business could turn its public gratitude feed on or off. People could choose to post a thank-you when a business helped them, with moderation and privacy controls. The sample feed on this page is only an illustration; posting is not live.

How will recipient stories be handled?

Stories should be shared only when the person chooses to share them. The idea is to celebrate what generosity made possible while keeping people’s privacy and dignity at the center.
